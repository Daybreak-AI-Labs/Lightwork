Trust receipts

A receipt is a portable, signed artifact for one audited decision — the row, the chain that follows it, and the commitment that fixed it — that an auditor, a regulator, or your customer's customer verifies offline with a public key obtained out of band. Mint one here, hand out the standalone verifier, or check a receipt someone sent you.

What a receipt proves

Three depths that are not synonyms. A verifier recomputes the claim from the row, so a stated claim that drifts from the row fails however validly it was re-signed. A co-located key reads as integrity (against accidental or non-privileged edits), an off-host key as tamper-evidence.

  • signeda genuine row, signed by the issuer's key
  • chainedprovably linked to a tip the issuer committed
  • anchoredthat commitment matched a digest the verifier brought from outside the receipt
