01 The problem

Agents pass the demo. They fail the diligence review.

In regulated work the blocker isn't capability. It's three questions no one can answer, so the project never leaves the pilot.

Q1

“What did it do?”

No tamper-evident record of the actions an agent took.

Q2

“Could it exfiltrate our data?”

No egress control, a black box, or a framework you secure yourself.

Q3

“Will it get worse?”

No proof it's improving instead of quietly drifting.

02 How it works

Not a chatbot. Not a framework. A governed runtime.

Every action an agent takes (every tool call, every payment, every write) passes through one gate before it happens. Then it lands where a human signs off.

01
Capability
Attenuate-only tokens. An agent can narrow its rights, never widen them.
02
Policy
Allow, deny, or require a human, bound to every action.
03
Shield
Scans for the dangerous action and the prompt injection.
04
Budget
Hard caps on tokens, dollars, wall-clock, and tools.
05
Audit
A signed, hash-chained entry you can verify offline.

Runs in your VPC, fully air-gapped, or on infrastructure Daybreak Labs hosts for you, with the same gates, the same record, and your own keys either way.

03 The company brain

Cortex. Everything your company knows, in one governed mind.

Cortex is the memory the whole workforce runs on: your documents and data, the decisions people made, the lessons the agents learned, and the Operating Record of everything that happened. Every agent, yours or a vendor's, reads from it and writes to it under audit. It is tenant-exact, encrypted with your keys, and never pooled across customers. Your company gets smarter; nobody else gets your company.

06 New offering

Autonomous Shoring. The fifth way to source work.

Offshore, nearshore, reshore, onshore. Each one moved work to a place. Autonomous Shoring moves a whole function to a governed agentic workforce, in your environment or ours, with a transition plan, service levels, and a record an auditor can verify. It is the offering for the work you would otherwise send to a BPO, and it replaces that decision instead of relocating it.

01

Cost and control, together

Offshoring traded control for cost, which is why reshoring happened. Policy is bound to every action and the record is yours, so the cost case and the control case finally point the same way.

02

A transition, not a prompt

The knowledge transfer a BPO takes months to run: watch a person do the job, and the agent factory synthesizes the governed pack that does it, clamped to a least-privilege envelope and approved by a human before it runs.

03

The number the CFO asks for

A Shoring P&L from real completed work: agent cost against your own fully-loaded human rate, throughput per department, every deliverable traceable in the Operating Record.
